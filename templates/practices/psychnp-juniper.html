02 What to Expect

Your First Visit

Knowing what happens next can make the first appointment easier. Here is how it usually goes.

  1. 1
    Step one

    Before Your Visit

    Send a request and we will call to find a time that suits you. You will get a short intake form, and we confirm your coverage before you come in.

  2. 2
    Step two · 60 minutes

    The Evaluation

    We talk through what is going on, your health history, sleep and daily life, and what you hope will change. Bring your questions and a list of current medications.

  3. 3
    Step three

    Your Plan

    We decide next steps together. That may include medication, lab work, a therapy referral, or simply more time to watch and wait. You will leave knowing the why behind each step.

  4. 4
    Step four

    Follow-Ups

    Shorter visits, in person or by video, to see how you are feeling and adjust. As things settle, visits usually become less frequent.

07 Questions

Common Questions

Something else on your mind? Call or send a request and we will be glad to help.

What is a psychiatric nurse practitioner?

A psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP) is an advanced practice nurse trained to evaluate, diagnose and treat mental health conditions, including prescribing medication. Elena holds a Doctor of Nursing Practice degree and is board certified.

Do you offer therapy?

My focus is evaluation and medication management. Many patients also see a therapist, and with your permission I am happy to coordinate with them. If you do not have one, I can suggest some options.

Do I need a referral?

Usually not, though some insurance plans ask for one. We will check this for you when we confirm your coverage.

Can I do every visit by video?

Many patients do. Some situations, such as certain medications or a physical exam, may call for an in-person visit. We will talk about what makes sense for you.

What if I need help right away?

This practice is not set up for emergencies. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency.
